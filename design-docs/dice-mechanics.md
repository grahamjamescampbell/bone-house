# Dice Mechanics

## Starter dice

The player starts with 5 dice, all the same. There are five icons spread around the six sides. They are:

Gold - Appears twice, allowing for more chance to earn this basic currency. The only icon that shows twice on these dice. Gold is used to bank to pay off the player's immense debt, but also banked to spend at the Dice Merchant to acquire more dice.

Favor - The second currency, used at the Dice Merchant to more specialised items.

Blade - An attack used against the player's opponent.

Ward - A defence used against an opponent's attack.

Skull - Three skulls in a single round busts the entire hand and the player receives none of the benefits the other dice might have otherwise given them. The player will also still receive any valid attacks from their opponents. The exact mechanics of skulls rolled after the initial roll is to be decided. Skulls rolled as part of the initial roll have no other impact other than taking up a slot that could have been something useful. By default players cannot reroll skulls, though other items may be acquired later in the game to either reroll skulls or to make skull acquisition more desirable.

## Beyond the Starter dice

In time the player can purchase more dice but also upgrades to allow them to carry and use more dice at each table.

The exact upper limits of these are not yet known, but the goal of the game is certainly to allow the player to start to customise their dice beyond the Starter loadout.

The player can only customise dice by unlocking and purchasing the tools to do so, but also by earning more dice to sacrifice in the customising process.

## Five dice

It is expected that the player will always have a minimum of five dice.

## Customising dice

The player must visit the Dice Merchant and use Favor to purchase the tools and materials needed to customise a die.

The exact descriptions of these tools is not yet known.

One of the materials the player must use each time they customise a die is another die. A target die to be customised will have a face selected to be replaced. The sacrificial die will have a face selected to be extracted, and that face will be placed in the location indicated on the target die.

This whole process can only take place away from the gaming tables in some yet to be determined location. The player cannot customise dice while at a table and engaged in the main play loop.

## Dice theme per floor

Each floor of this gambling den is modelled after the 9 hells from Dante's Inferno. Therefore, each floor's dealers has a theme of dice appropriate to that level of hell.

It won't be so explicit as to just say "This is Limbo, the first circle of hell from Dante's Inferno, and therefore the dice on this floor are all XYZ".

But there will be some way of indicating that the floors all correspond somehow to the circles.

## Boss dice

These are unique dice given up only by the Boss Table on each floor. Players play through the other tables on the floor to reach the Boss Table to attempt to take on the Floor Boss.

Each Floor Boss is modelled closely on the guardian of that circle of hell, and has a die they reward the player when victorious that similiarly speaks to their character and style.

The exact nature of each Floor Boss is not yet known.

## Modifying the same die multiple times

A material cost must be paid each time a die is remodelled. The cost increases considerably each time, the fiction giving the reason that a die becomes less stable the more it is meddled with, so it needs ever more care and attention paid to it to ensure it doesn't just turn to dust.

It is entirely in the gify of the game to allow the player to create a die that has nothing but blades on it, thereby guaranteeing that at least one blade will always roll and therefore one attack is likely. Similiarly gold or any other face.

This same tenet applies to the Boss Dice. When acquired, they player can add a Boss Die to their loadout and use its unique abilities to help them in subsequent games. But they can also extract a face from the Boss Die to apply to another die, thereby giving that target die some of the essence of the boss die.

If one face of a Boss Die is particularly appealing to the player, there is nothing to stop them from facing the Floor Boss again and again to acquire more of their Boss Die and eventually create a powerful custom die that is nothing but a specific (and possibly powerful) face from that Boss Die.

Doing so would require a lot of resources, and also beating the Floor Boss at least 6 times to ensure that all six sides of a custom die are completely covered in the desired sides from that Boss Die. It's a time commitment as it's not just the Floor Boss the player would need to beat but also the other tables on the Floor to gather up the materials needed to do the customisation multiple times over. But, it does grant an incredibly powerful die which is something I'm keen to make available to the player to make them feel like they've broken the game.

## Special dice effects

This is tricky and needs a lot of thought, but I expect that the Boss Dice in particular will provide ample opportunity to develop interesting additional effects beyond simply offering Blades, Wards, Coins and Favors.

For example, the idea that a Blade could also be set on fire somehow - or all blades rolled are also set on fire because of a specific face that shows up on a Boss Die. Thereby giving the player more damage to deal, and also apply a damage over time effect on the current opponent.

Another example is a face that triples damage if three Blades are rolled. This encourages the player to Push each roll, but also insists that the player spend time and resouces in expanding their hand beyond the initial 5 dice to allow for more chance of more blades being rolled. More dice though of course means that other negatives could also come up more often, so there's balance to be struck there.

Some of that balance could be achieved with other boons, perhaps purchased from the Bone Merchant. But I won't investigate that here.

The exact nature of these special dice effects needs to be carefully considered.
